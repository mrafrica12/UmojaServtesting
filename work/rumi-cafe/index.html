The challenge

The experience was already there. The digital visibility needed to catch up.

Rumi Café already had the kind of experience customers were willing to recommend. The opportunity was to make that reputation easier to discover online.

The goal wasn't simply to build another café website. It was to create a stronger connection between Google, Maps, customer reviews, local search and Rumi's own website — so people searching for coffee in Kilimani could more easily discover, understand and visit the business.

Where we started

Google was telling customers the café was closed.

3
Google ReviewsRecorded on the Rumi Café Google Business Profile, 10 May 2026

On 10 May 2026 the café's Google listing was unclaimed, carried three reviews, and was marked Temporarily closed. For anyone searching for coffee in Kilimani, the business was effectively invisible — and for anyone who did find it, the profile said not to bother going.

The café itself was open and busy. The gap was entirely between the experience customers were having in the room and what Google was telling everyone else.

  • Unclaimed. The profile showed a “Claim this business” prompt, so nobody could respond to reviews, fix the hours or post updates.
  • Marked “Temporarily closed”. The single most damaging thing a profile can say about a business that is, in fact, open.
  • 3 reviews. Almost no social proof for a café whose customers were already enthusiastic in person.
  • Incomplete business information. The listing carried a different contact number and a map pin that did not match the Titan Complex entrance.

  • Claiming the profile

    The listing was unclaimed and marked “Temporarily closed”. Claiming it, correcting the status, fixing the contact number and moving the map pin to the Titan Complex entrance came before anything else — none of the rest works while Google is telling customers the café is shut.

  • Customer reviews — learned in the room

    This part did not happen over email, and it was not a flown-in workshop either. UmojaServ was in Nairobi on other work and spent a few hours at Rumi most mornings and evenings across nine days — partly because the coffee is worth the trip. That was enough time to watch how the owner and the floor staff actually talked to their customers. A café runs two different rooms in a day — the morning coffee run and the slower evening — and the moment to ask someone for a review is not the same in both.

    What came out of it was an ask the team could already make in their own words: an honest review of the place and the coffee, the same request whatever kind of visit it had been, with nothing offered in exchange. Staff ask; the customer decides whether to write, and what to say.

    Then the owner replies — to every review, positive or negative, personally and by name. That is the step most businesses skip, and it is visible on the profile today: each reply picks up something specific the customer actually mentioned.

    UmojaServ does not write, buy, filter or incentivise reviews. Every review counted on this page was written by a customer of Rumi Café.

Rumi Café's mobile Lighthouse Performance score increased from 72 to 90 while preserving the site's design, imagery, functionality and SEO. First Contentful Paint improved from 3.8 seconds to 1.7 seconds, while Speed Index improved from 5.2 seconds to 2.0 seconds. SEO remained at 100.

What changed

  • Mobile performance audit. Traced what was holding back the first screen on a phone before changing anything.
  • Above-the-fold loading. The opening section stayed invisible until JavaScript had run. Its entrance animation now runs in CSS from the first paint — same look, no wait.
  • Critical resource prioritisation. The styles the homepage needs are delivered with the page instead of as a render-blocking stylesheet; fonts are self-hosted and load after the first paint, with size-matched fallbacks so text doesn't jump.
  • Image optimisation. Phones no longer download the full-size Ginger Rush photo, and homepage photos are re-encoded as AVIF with no visible loss of quality.
  • Third-party requests deferred. The live Google rating on the page is now fetched after the page has loaded.
  • Accessibility fix. Added the homepage's missing main landmark — Accessibility 98 → 100.
  • Design checked, then retested. Every page was compared screen-by-screen at nine widths from 320 to 1440 pixels to confirm nothing visible changed, then re-run in PageSpeed Insights.

Tests performed using Google PageSpeed Insights / Lighthouse under simulated mobile conditions. Lighthouse scores can vary between test runs. At the time of testing, Google PageSpeed Insights did not report sufficient field data for real-user Core Web Vitals.

Before and after are single PageSpeed Insights runs on rumicafe.co.ke on 25 September 2026 (Lighthouse 13.5.0, emulated Moto G Power, slow 4G throttling). Cumulative Layout Shift is reported as recorded: 0 before, 0.005 after.
