Its website helps families understand their care options and request support. Behind that customer experience, the team coordinates caregivers, shifts and timekeeping.

The website · Before and after

A clearer way for families to start.

The previous homepage led with a broad message and a single "Find Care" button.

The updated experience starts with the family's situation. Whether someone is arranging care for a parent, spouse, themselves or someone else, the first step is clear: "Who needs care?"

A tap-to-call phone number and Maryland licence information keep the next steps and essential business details within easy reach.

Implementation note. The previous page also exposed repeated server-configuration text ("Order allow,deny Deny from all") at the top of the page. That issue is no longer present in the updated experience.

Navigation

Menus that answer the first questions.

Families usually need to know three things first:

  • What help is available?
  • Do you serve my area?
  • How do I get started?

The updated navigation organises the experience around those questions while keeping the phone number and "Request care" action within easy reach.

The Service Areas menu makes local coverage easier to understand by showing the communities served alongside the four Maryland counties behind them.

Connected operations

The website was only the beginning.

Alongside the public website, UmojaServ built a role-based staff workspace around the people running the operation.

Administrators, schedulers and caregivers sign into the same system but see tools designed around their responsibilities.

See the operation at a glance.

Today's shifts, open shifts, pending requests, clock-in exceptions, caregiver assignments, timesheets and qualification alerts surface in one operational view. The goal is not to give administrators more software to manage. It is to make the issues requiring attention easier to see.

Know what needs attention today.

Schedulers get a focused view of shifts, staffing, requests and timekeeping without navigating the full administrative workspace. The workspace centers the information needed to coordinate the day.

Everything needed for the workday.

Caregivers see their own schedule, open shifts, availability, notifications and hours, with clock-in and clock-out centered around the shift workflow.

The screenshots show the portal interface with no shifts or hours loaded. They illustrate the workspaces, not the agency's activity.

What we did

Built around the family — and the people delivering the care.

  • A homepage that starts the request. A "Who needs care?" first step gives families a clear place to begin, with tap-to-call access and essential agency information close by.
  • Navigation around real questions. Services, service areas, how it works, resources and contact are organised around what families need to understand before requesting care.
  • Built to be fast on a phone. The new website was engineered around mobile performance and tested at 100 for Performance in the captured Google PageSpeed Insights mobile run.
  • Role-based staff workspaces. Separate Admin, Scheduler and Caregiver experiences support scheduling, availability, timekeeping, timesheets and day-to-day operational oversight.
