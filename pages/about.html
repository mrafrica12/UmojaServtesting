Our Mission

Built in Africa.
Connected beyond borders.

Digital business systems for African businesses and the diaspora.

UmojaServ comes from umoja, Swahili for unity, and serv, for service to others. That's the job, really: getting small businesses and the internet to work together without the owner having to become a tech person.

We grew up watching our parents, aunts and neighbours run businesses with their hands. The tailor who knew your measurements without writing them down. The salon owner who remembered birthdays. The mechanic who could hear what was wrong with an engine. The café owner who'd start your order before you sat down.

Brilliant businesses. Almost none of them online.

That's still true. Your customers are looking for you on their phones, and too many good businesses are hard to find, hard to reach, or juggling apps that make more work, not less.

We don't think the answer is big software built for Silicon Valley. It's tools you can use on your busiest day: WhatsApp, Google, a spreadsheet you own.

The business already has the knowledge. Our job is to help the technology catch up.

Why I started UmojaServ

I'm a Google Local Guide, Level 8. I've added more than 3,700 reviews, photos and edits to Google Maps, and they've been viewed over 29 million times.

Somewhere along the way it clicked. Most small business owners have no idea how much those reviews matter to people like me deciding where to go next.

So why not teach them? And while I'm at it, build them a simple website connected to the tools they already use. Most of those tools are free: a Google account and WhatsApp. Get them found. Get them organised.

Rumi Café in Kilimani, Nairobi is the clearest example. In May 2026, Google said it was temporarily closed. It wasn't. It was open and busy, with three reviews on an unclaimed listing.

I was in Nairobi on other work and ended up there most mornings and evenings for nine days. Partly to watch how the staff talked to customers, partly because the coffee deserved a round two. And a round nine.

We claimed the listing, fixed the status and the map pin, and worked out with the team how to ask for an honest review, with nothing offered in return. The owner replies to every one. By 17 October 2026, Rumi had 119 reviews at 4.9 stars.

That's the whole idea, in one café. Read the Rumi Café case study →

Where we work

We started in East Africa. Now we also work with businesses in Atlanta and across the U.S., many of them diaspora-owned. Nairobi, Zanzibar, Dar es Salaam, Kampala, Addis Ababa or Atlanta, the job is the same: make it easy for customers to find you, trust you, reach you and come back.

Umoja in Action

Websites are one part of what we do.

We've also donated soccer gear and worked with youth clubs in Zanzibar, Ghana, Cuba and Kenya, including Blue Bentos FC in Nairobi.

I've coached youth soccer since 2005. Teaching and football have always gone together for me, and so has a simple belief: what we do for other people matters more than what we do for ourselves. Nobody's in this world alone. When I can help, I help.

The best part hasn't changed in all those years. A kid kicking a ball that travelled miles and miles to reach their pitch. Nothing replaces that.
